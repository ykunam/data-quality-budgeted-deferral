\noindent\textit{Short communication.}

\begin{abstract}
AI governance must allocate scarce review capacity when data-quality rules prohibit automation. We investigate when quality information improves deferral beyond model probabilities, separating estimation, admission and rule enforcement. A fixed-loss allocator handles mandatory overload exactly; propensity-weighted fitting addresses selective review logs under explicit overlap assumptions. Following exploratory credit experiments, we froze a validation plan before analyzing a new Bank Marketing dataset. In its primary contrast, adding quality only to the automation-loss head reduced cost by 5.23\% relative to probability-only estimation under informative missingness (20/20 splits; corrected 95\% interval for absolute reduction, 0.0099--0.0224; $p<0.001$). This supports a conditional benefit, not universal superiority. Across six planned secondary comparisons, inverse-propensity weighting did not significantly improve cost after Holm adjustment. A separate transfer check uses recorded human image classifications without simulating responses to corrupted stimuli. Policy ablations distinguish visible-defect coverage from zero rule violations; service simulations distinguish review priority from capacity under unit service times. The contribution is a reproducible conditions analysis of governance-constrained deferral, with matched component comparisons and an independently evaluated quality hypothesis.
\end{abstract}
\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} Knowledge-based systems; Learning to defer; Data quality; AI governance; Human--AI collaboration; Review capacity; Managerial decision-making

\section{Introduction}
Organizations divide decisions between models and reviewers. Governance assigns responsibilities and controls \cite{abraham,janssen,mikalefgov,wirtz}, but oversight alone does not establish effective control \cite{green}. Deteriorating data can increase mandatory review; managers must distinguish predictive benefit, rule coverage and service capacity.

Rejection and learning to defer trade model error against abstention or reviewer error \cite{chow,zhangreject,mozannar,verma}; uncertainty methods offer simpler alternatives \cite{mena,abdar}. Triage separates model and human error \cite{raghu}. DeCCaF already combines costs, limited feedback and workload constraints \cite{alves}; related work addresses sparse expert predictions \cite{hemmer}, quality-aware cascades \cite{farinhas} and congestion \cite{congestion}. We test quality inputs under rules that prohibit automation and require holds under overload, claiming neither a new surrogate nor the first constrained deferral system.

Completeness and validity are measurable but incomplete proxies for quality \cite{chai,iot,mohammed}. Human-in-the-loop and knowledge-based decision systems connect predictions to action \cite{wuhitl,tariq,onari}; routing traces document decisions rather than explain the underlying model \cite{arrieta,adadi,artelt}. We ask: \emph{under what data-quality and feedback conditions does learned deferral improve on probability-only estimation and cost-aware heuristics under mandatory review rules?} We provide exact allocation, matched comparisons and planned cross-dataset validation.

\section{Method}
\subsection{Rules and scarce review capacity}
A predictor gives $p_i$ and automates with $\hat y_i=\mathbf1[p_i\ge1/6]$. False-negative, false-positive, review and hold costs are $5$, $1$, $c_R=0.05$ and $c_H=1.5$. Observable quality $q_i=(u_i,v_i)$ is the missing-field and out-of-range fraction. Training-only bounds are $[Q_{.01,j}-.1t_j,Q_{.99,j}+.1t_j]$, $t_j=\max(Q_{.99,j}-Q_{.01,j},1)$. The knowledge base marks $m_i=\mathbf1[u_i\ge.5\lor v_i\ge.25]$. These are explicit experimental policy assumptions, not regulatory thresholds or empirically optimal rules.

Let $a_i,r_i,h_i$ denote automate, review and hold, $M=\{i:m_i=1\}$, and $B=\lfloor bn\rfloor$. For fixed estimated automation and reviewer losses, allocate by
\begin{equation}
\min\frac1n\sum_i\left[a_i\hat\ell_i^A+r_i(\hat\ell_i^H+c_R)+h_ic_H\right],
\quad \sum_i h_i=(|M|-B)_+,
\label{eq:allocation}
\end{equation}
subject to $a_i+r_i+h_i=1$, $\sum_i r_i\le B$, $a_i\le1-m_i$ and $h_i\le m_i$, with binary actions. The equality assumes mandatory service priority, not an unconstrained economic optimum.

\textbf{Proposition 1.} If $|M|\le B$, review all mandatory cases and up to $B-|M|$ optional cases with largest positive $\hat g_i=\hat\ell_i^A-\hat\ell_i^H-c_R$. If $|M|>B$, review the $B$ mandatory cases with smallest $\hat\ell_i^H$ and hold the rest. \emph{Proof.} Optional review changes cost by $-\hat g_i/n$. Under overload, only the sum of reviewed human losses varies; $(|M|-B)c_H$ is constant. Exchange arguments establish both cases. Allocation takes $O(n\log n)$. Exactness is conditional on estimated losses, not a guarantee of minimum realized cost.

Under mandatory overload, changing only the automation-loss head cannot alter routing with a fixed reviewer head. Quality must improve reviewer-loss estimates or policy coverage instead.

\textbf{Proposition 2.} With fixed mandatory flags, identical arrivals, unit review times, no abandonment and work-conserving mandatory service, queue ordering leaves mandatory backlog unchanged. \emph{Proof.} The recurrence $Q_{t+1}=(Q_t+A_t^M-B_t)_+$ depends on counts, not identities. This does not extend automatically to heterogeneous service times, changing flags or reviewer fatigue.

\subsection{Loss estimation and selective feedback}
With $w(y)=5y+(1-y)$, targets are $L^A=w(y)\mathbf1[\hat y\ne y]$ and $L^H=w(y)\mathbf1[\mathrm{review}\ne y]$. All gain-fitting rows have outcome labels, but only logged rows have a reviewer decision. Two boosted regressors estimate the losses. Probability inputs are $(p,s)$, $s=\min(5p,1-p)$; quality inputs add $(u,v,pu,pv,\hat yu,\hat yv)$. The primary ablation changes only the automation head, retaining the same probability-only reviewer head, admission rule and allocator.

Let $S$ indicate logging with known propensity $\pi(W)$ for observed covariates $W$. Unweighted fitting minimizes logged-row squared error; inverse-propensity weighting (IPW) uses weights $1/\pi(W)$, normalized to mean one among logged rows \cite{ipw}. Under $S\perp L^H\mid W$ and $\pi(W)>0$, $\mathbb E[SR_f/\pi(W)]=\mathbb E[R_f]$, $R_f=\{L^H-f(Z)\}^2$. This standard identity identifies fitting risk even if $Z$ omits logging covariates; it does not guarantee finite-sample benefit or correct hidden selection, missing outcomes or test shift. ChatGPT and Claude assisted code and analysis development; numerical results were computed with the released Python implementation.

\section{Experimental design}
\subsection{Exploration and prospective validation}
The exploratory credit grid uses German, Australian, Default and HMEQ \cite{lessmann,german,australian,default,hmeq}, simulated reviewers and nine quality conditions. Its published-surrogate adaptations are retained as exploratory evidence; they are not newly tuned state-of-the-art comparisons. No learned router beat the best heuristic when that grid was pooled, and its 18 quality-ablation tests did not survive Holm adjustment.

Before fetching new validation data, we committed the primary contrast, secondary family and complete design (plan \texttt{656f527}, implementation \texttt{da41f59}). Default supplies development evidence; Bank Marketing supplies a new business-domain validation \cite{bank} (45,211 rows). We remove post-call \texttt{duration}; categorical mappings use predictor-training rows only, with unknown or unseen categories treated as missing. Historical subscription labels are real, but review outcomes and costs remain simulated. This is cross-domain validation of the routing experiment, not a credit-deployment study.

Feature-identical rows stay together in partitions of 45\% training, 15\% calibration, 20\% gain fitting and 20\% testing (seeds 2--21). Histogram gradient boosting (at most 120 iterations, 15 leaves) is logistically calibrated on clean/missing/outlier/mixed copies; gain rows receive the same augmentation. Heads choose three or seven leaves by grouped 80/20 inner gain validation (100 iterations, rate .06, L2=5), then refit. No augmented copies cross inner folds; test outcomes never select models.

Conditions are clean, informative missingness, stale values and severe defects. Informative missingness multiplies a uniform $[0,.35]$ rate by 1.8 for positives and .6 for negatives. Stale continuous values are multiplied by $[.55,.90]$ on half the rows. Severe shift combines 35--75\% missingness and 8--25\% outliers. Reviewers have constant error .10 or AI-correlated error, $\min(.45,.05+.25\mathbf1[\hat y\ne y]+.10[1-|2p_{clean}-1|])$. These mechanisms are illustrative; neither reviewer is defined from quality inputs.

\subsection{Logging, matched comparisons and human transfer}
Logging is random ($\pi=.4$), uncertainty-prioritized or rule-prioritized. Selective policies use $\pi=.1+.8\sigma(\alpha+z)$ with mean .4, where $z$ is standardized threshold proximity or $4m+2(u+v)$. Shared uniforms and reviewer outcomes give methods identical logs. Comparators are probability-only, quality-in-automation-only, quality-in-both-heads, observed-feature two-head, single-head gain, confidence, Chow and rules-only. The feature comparator includes raw inputs and quality; every head has the same two-option search.

The matched arm shares probability-only reviewer-loss ordering under overload. A fill-budget arm removes optional admission cutoffs. Native, gain-order and no-rule arms isolate allocation and rules at 10\% and 20\% capacity.

CIFAR-10H supplies recorded human judgments \cite{human}; ten-class choices are mapped to animal/vehicle. The predictor uses $8\times8$ averaged RGB features on 10,000 original images. Gain fitting samples one recorded choice per image; held-out empirical human error supplies evaluation loss. No corruption is applied: people saw clean images. Logging, head search and 20\% capacity are unchanged. This is neither finance-expert validation nor a test of the primary quality hypothesis.

\subsection{Endpoints and inference}
The single primary test compares quality-in-automation-only with probability-only on Bank under informative missingness, random logging and 20\% capacity, averaging reviewers per split. Success requires positive reduction, two-sided $p<.05$ and at least 1\% relative improvement. Corrected resampled tests use ratio $.2/.8$ \cite{nadeau}; splits are dependent. Six secondary tests compare IPW versus unweighted full-quality fitting for two datasets and three logging policies, averaging conditions/reviewers at 20\%, with one Holm adjustment \cite{holm}. Other analyses are exploratory; ambiguous earlier seven-/nine-test claims are retired.

\section{Results and discussion}
\textbf{A conditional quality benefit was confirmed.} The planned contrast reduced cost by \PrimaryRelative\ (absolute reduction \PrimaryDiff; corrected 95\% interval \PrimaryCI; $p<.001$; 20/20 splits), meeting the 1\% practical criterion (Table~\ref{tab:inference}). Reviewer estimation and mandatory ordering are fixed; the fill-budget arm retains the same reduction. Figure~\ref{fig:quality} reports all condition/logging cells. Under mandatory overload, the automation-head effect is zero, as predicted. Confirmation concerns this synthetic mechanism on new data, not universal superiority.

\textbf{Knowledge rules have costs and blind spots.} Rules-only routing, no-rule learned routing and their combination separate policy enforcement from predictive benefit. \RuleResultSentence\ A rule can be perfectly obeyed while missing stale but plausible values. These metrics concern the declared corruption and policy assumptions, not legal compliance or complete data-quality assurance.

\textbf{Capacity results require their assumptions.} The 40-day simulation introduces severe defects on days 11--20 and carries holds forward. Service counts all eligible mandatory arrivals, with unresolved cases unmet; quantiles account for censoring \cite{kaplan}. Cost ordering leaves 21.7\% open at 20\% capacity, none at 40\%. At 40\%, FIFO lowers the 95th-percentile delay from 13.9 to 7.1 days despite identical backlog counts (Fig.~\ref{fig:service}). This depends on unit service and fixed flags.

Managers should validate quality features against a local cost-aware heuristic, document policy provenance, maintain exploration where review logs are selective, and monitor unresolved cohorts as well as rule violations. Organizational complementarity \cite{jarrahi} and AI capability \cite{capability} motivate these decisions, but firm-performance or causal managerial effects are not tested here.

\section{Limitations and conclusion}
Bank is new to this project but old and synthetically corrupted. Human images do not validate finance workflows, damaged stimuli or fatigue. Costs and thresholds need domain validation; gradual drift \cite{lu}, heterogeneous service, fairness and missing outcomes remain untested. IPW does not correct hidden selection. Published surrogate adaptations remain exploratory; no newly tuned DeCCaF comparison or new learning guarantee is claimed.

A frozen validation supports a conditional quality benefit, null weighting results and no universal superiority. Separating estimation, rules and capacity makes these boundaries observable.
